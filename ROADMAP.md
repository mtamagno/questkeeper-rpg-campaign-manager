# QuestKeeper Roadmap

> **Your character. Your party. Every legendary session.**

QuestKeeper is becoming more than a campaign archive. Our destination is a
complete home for tabletop adventures: useful before game night, alive during
the session and worth revisiting years after the final roll.

This is an intentionally ambitious twelve-month roadmap. AI-assisted
development helps us move quickly, but everything generated from notes,
transcripts or prompts remains reviewable by a human before publication.

**Roadmap window:** October 2026–November 2027<br>
**Current stable release:** QuestKeeper 1.1.5 · Storyforge LTS<br>
**Next major release:** QuestKeeper 1.5 · Game Night Edition · January 2027<br>
**Annual destination:** QuestKeeper 2.0 · November 2027

The milestones describe what players and Game Masters should experience—not the
technology hidden behind the screen. Their order may change in response to real
campaigns and community feedback.

Delivery is governed by the [working agreement](docs/WORKING-AGREEMENT.md) and
a documented [weekly review](docs/templates/WEEKLY-REVIEW.md), so the roadmap
can evolve without losing decisions, quality or accountability.

---

## October 2026 · The party finds a home

The first visit should immediately explain why QuestKeeper belongs at the table.

- Separate guided paths for players and Game Masters.
- A polished sample campaign that can be explored without installing anything.
- Memorable character profiles with portraits, biographies, secrets and growth.
- Direct access to the latest chapter, party bonds and explored places.
- A welcoming mobile experience for the inevitable pre-session phone check.
- An installable Companion that can reopen recently visited material offline.

**Checkpoint:** a new visitor understands QuestKeeper in under two minutes and
can try the complete player journey immediately.

---

## 4–17 November 2026 · Every page earns its place

QuestKeeper pauses feature expansion to review the entire Companion and
Campaign Manager page by page. This is a product checkpoint, not a cosmetic
intermission.

- Every page makes its purpose, primary action and reading order obvious.
- Dense information uses deliberate grouping and progressive disclosure rather
  than long, confusing walls of controls or cards.
- Typography, spacing, imagery and empty space feel intentional instead of
  approximate or unfinished.
- Keyboard, screen-reader, contrast, zoom, touch, reduced-motion, loading and
  error states receive the same care as the default desktop view.
- The public example, completed Phandelver and active Curse of Strahd prove that
  improvements work for both clean and content-heavy campaigns.
- The local Campaign Manager and guided setup receive the same review as the
  player-facing Companion.

**Checkpoint:** every route and workspace has recorded evidence, no unowned
blocker or high-severity accessibility defect remains, and later features inherit
a clearer, calmer foundation.

See the complete
[UI, UX and accessibility quality fortnight](docs/UI-UX-ACCESSIBILITY-FORTNIGHT.md).

---

## November–December 2026 · Characters become living stories

A character should feel like a person whose history grows with every session,
not a collection of numbers frozen on one sheet.

- Level-by-level evolution with clear comparisons between chapters.
- Better spell, ability, equipment, currency and resource views.
- Personal journals for thoughts, sketches, letters and hidden memories.
- Favourite moments, meaningful objects and relationships on the public profile.
- Beautiful printable and shareable character dossiers.
- Campaign-aware portraits, covers and visual references that remain consistent.
- Fast character lookup and a “return to my character” shortcut.

**Checkpoint:** players return to their profile between sessions because it
contains something a normal character sheet cannot preserve.

---

## January 2027 · QuestKeeper 1.5 · Game Night Edition

QuestKeeper becomes the page the group opens when everyone sits down to play.

- A game-night dashboard with party status, current location and open threads.
- A five-minute catch-up beside the complete written chronicle.
- Pinned clues, promises, quests and unresolved mysteries.
- A campaign-wide search that understands people, places, sessions and clues.
- Recently visited pages, bookmarks and a continue-reading shortcut.
- Printable game-night summaries for tables that prefer paper.
- Smooth desktop, mobile, keyboard and offline use.

**Checkpoint:** the group can resume an adventure after a long break without
digging through chat logs or asking the Game Master to retell everything.

---

## February 2027 · The chronicle comes alive

Session notes evolve into an illustrated history of decisions and consequences.

- A visual campaign timeline spanning the complete adventure.
- Key choices connected to the characters who made them.
- Quotes, turning points and memorable failures preserved alongside victories.
- Session chapters connected directly to relevant maps, NPCs and relationships.
- “Previously on…” presentations for the beginning of a session.
- Story collections for arcs, chapters and completed quests.
- A guided Campaign Book studio for finished adventures, with a cover, title
  page, cast, table of contents, chapters, artwork, appendices, epilogues and
  credits.
- Four authoring paths for the final book: fully manual, semi-automatic with
  review checkpoints, automatic with local AI, or automatic with an explicitly
  configured remote AI provider.
- Page-by-page editing, selective regeneration and visible source notes before
  the Game Master approves the final text.
- A print-ready PDF and a screen-friendly digital edition that remain part of
  the portable campaign archive.

**Checkpoint:** reading the chronicle feels like revisiting the campaign rather
than browsing a folder of summaries, and a completed adventure can become a
book the group is proud to keep.

---

## March 2027 · A world that remembers

Every name, clue and location becomes part of one connected world.

- Rich NPC dossiers showing encounters, loyalties, secrets and changing opinions.
- Interactive party and NPC relationship histories.
- Maps with discovered locations, notes and session-linked markers.
- A clue board connecting mysteries, evidence, suspects and unresolved questions.
- Quest and promise tracking without turning the campaign into a task manager.
- Search paths that lead naturally from a remembered detail to its complete story.
- Spoiler-aware views so public discoveries and Game Master knowledge stay distinct.

**Checkpoint:** when a player remembers half a name from six months ago,
QuestKeeper can lead the party back to the moment it mattered.

---

## April 2027 · The Session Scribe matures

Whether the group plays on Discord or around a physical table, recording a
session should require less effort than remembering it unaided.

- Guided Discord setup and clear consent controls.
- More reliable speaker recognition using the names the party actually uses.
- Reviewable transcripts with quick speaker corrections and removable mistakes.
- Automatic suggestions for key moments, quotes, NPCs and locations.
- Draft recaps in different lengths: quick catch-up, full chronicle and GM notes.
- Photo and text import for handwritten or separately typed notes.
- A final review desk where nothing is published without approval.

**Checkpoint:** one evening of play can become a useful draft chronicle in
minutes, while the group keeps control of what is saved.

---

## May 2027 · The Game Master's studio

Maintaining the campaign becomes a guided creative workflow instead of file
management.

- A campaign creation journey from first idea to published Companion.
- Live previews while creating characters, sessions, locations and NPCs.
- A media library for portraits, maps, handouts and galleries.
- Campaign health checks that explain what is missing and how to fix it.
- Reusable session structures for mysteries, journeys, social scenes and battles.
- Faster bulk changes when names, themes or translations evolve.
- Clear previews of what players can see and what remains behind the screen.

**Checkpoint:** a first-time Game Master can build a convincing campaign home
without editing data files or learning the project structure.

---

## June 2027 · Every player can contribute

The campaign archive should represent the whole party while the Game Master
retains editorial control.

- Player-submitted journal pages, character updates and favourite moments.
- A review inbox for accepting, editing or returning contributions.
- Personal versions of shared events: what each character believed happened.
- Party polls for names, memories and artwork without requiring permanent accounts.
- Shareable contribution packages for self-hosted campaigns.
- Optional end-of-session prompts that help players record feelings and intentions.
- Credits for the writers, artists and chroniclers behind a campaign.

**Checkpoint:** the Companion stops sounding like one narrator and begins to
feel like the memory of an entire party.

---

## July 2027 · Any language, any table

QuestKeeper becomes easier to adapt to different groups, systems and cultures.

- Complete interface translations that are separate from campaign translations.
- A clear view of missing or outdated translated passages.
- Shared review packages for friends helping with another language.
- Custom terminology for classes, abilities, chapters and game-specific concepts.
- Flexible character facts for campaigns that do not use the familiar six abilities.
- Right-to-left layout foundations and improved typography for more writing systems.
- Language choices remembered independently for every visitor.

**Checkpoint:** translating or adapting a campaign feels like part of the
creative process, not a workaround.

---

## August 2027 · Your chronicle, your style

Every campaign can express its own mood without requiring a designer.

- Guided visual themes for heroic fantasy, gothic horror, mystery and other tones.
- Campaign colours, typography, ornaments and card styles chosen from one studio.
- Multiple readable layouts for covers, profiles, journals and printable dossiers.
- Visual consistency guides for characters and recurring locations.
- Artwork briefs and prompt packs based on the current scene's mood.
- Dedicated notebook styles based on the writer's emotions and point of view.
- Accessible contrast checks built into visual choices.

**Checkpoint:** two QuestKeeper campaigns can feel completely different while
remaining equally readable and easy to use.

---

## September 2027 · A creative companion on your terms

Optional AI tools help with repetitive creative work without taking authorship
away from the group. Every workflow can remain manual, run privately on local
models or use a remote provider chosen explicitly by the campaign owner.

- Draft summaries generated from reviewed notes or transcripts.
- Suggestions for connections between characters, clues and earlier sessions.
- First drafts for NPC dossiers, location descriptions and image briefs.
- Consistency checks for names, dates, heights and known campaign facts.
- Private campaign questions answered from the group's own archive.
- Multiple writing tones that preserve the chosen narrator's voice.
- A visible source trail showing which notes supported each suggestion.
- Clear previews of what will leave the device before any remote AI request,
  with per-task consent and no silent provider fallback.

**Checkpoint:** AI saves preparation time while every published word remains a
deliberate choice by the group, and every group controls where processing
happens.

## October 2027 · QuestKeeper goes everywhere

A chronicle containing years of memories should feel safe and remain useful
beyond one computer. QuestKeeper also becomes a first-class mobile companion
instead of merely a desktop page that happens to shrink.

- Released Android and iOS apps for phones and tablets.
- A purpose-designed portrait experience for phones: fast character access,
  session catch-up, bookmarks, notes and reading without cramped desktop UI.
- A purpose-designed landscape experience for tablets: split navigation,
  readable character sheets, maps and a game-night dashboard suited to the
  table.
- Smooth hand-off between phone portrait, tablet landscape and desktop without
  losing the current campaign, character or reading position.
- Understandable backups with visible dates and contents.
- Preview-before-restore and recovery from accidental changes.
- Smooth upgrades from older QuestKeeper campaign formats.
- Complete campaign transfer between computers and hosting providers.
- Offline reading for saved characters, recaps and game-night material on every
  supported device.
- Shareable original campaign structures, themes and templates.
- A community showcase for legally redistributable examples and inspiration.

**Checkpoint:** players can rely on the released Android or iOS app at the
table—whether they carry a portrait phone or a landscape tablet—and groups
trust QuestKeeper because they can always understand, move and recover their
archive.

---

## November 2027 · QuestKeeper 2.0

QuestKeeper 2.0 unites the player Companion, Game Master's studio, Session
Scribe and optional local creative tools into one coherent journey.

The release is ready when:

- a new Game Master can create and publish a campaign in one evening;
- a player can reach their character, latest recap and active threads in seconds;
- the world connects sessions, people, places, relationships, clues and choices;
- player contributions are welcome without surrendering editorial control;
- campaigns remain useful online, on mobile, in print and during limited connectivity;
- backup, restoration and upgrades feel safe;
- Android and iOS feel intentional on portrait phones and landscape tablets;
- a finished campaign can become an editable, illustrated Campaign Book through
  manual, semi-automatic, local-AI or opt-in remote-AI authoring;
- the live demo honestly shows the complete journey before installation;
- the project remains self-hosted, account-free and respectful of copyrighted work.

**Checkpoint:** QuestKeeper feels less like software beside the campaign and
more like the book the party has been writing together.

## Principles that will not change

### The party owns its story

Campaign files remain portable and under the group's control. QuestKeeper does
not require a subscription or permanent player accounts.

### Players deserve more than a data sheet

Statistics matter, but so do memories, relationships, artwork, private thoughts
and the choices that changed the adventure.

### The Game Master stays in control

Generated drafts, transcripts, contributions and imported notes remain
suggestions until the Game Master reviews and publishes them.

### AI assists; people author

Automation accelerates transcription, organisation and drafting. It does not
silently rewrite the campaign or replace the voice of its players.

### Every table is different

QuestKeeper will remain adaptable to different languages, tones and tabletop
systems instead of becoming tied to one commercial setting.

### Original worlds belong here

The project will not bundle copied rulebooks, commercial adventures or artwork
that cannot be legally redistributed.

---

## Beyond 2.0

Ideas being explored for later journeys include shared live table displays,
campaign-to-campaign character legacies, optional collaborative hosting and a
larger ecosystem of community-made themes and original adventure frameworks.
They are directions, not promises, until the 2.0 foundations prove dependable.

This roadmap is a direction, not a locked quest log. Bug reports,
accessibility feedback and stories from real groups can change what comes first.
